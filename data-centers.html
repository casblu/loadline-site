1 · Why it matters

Transparency is how the public can weigh the trade-offs

Large, fast-growing loads

LBNL estimates US data centers used 176 TWh in 2023, 4.4% of US electricity. The IEA estimates 485 TWh worldwide in 2025. Both are modelled estimates, not metered totals (see grid context).

Local effects need local numbers

Water and power are drawn from specific grids and aquifers. Utilities, regulators and residents can only assess a project if site-level figures exist, and few operators publish them.

Disclosure is uneven and often old

Reports come once a year, months after the period ends. Definitions differ between companies. This tracker shows what exists, what it covers and how old it is, so disclosure can be compared and can improve.

Tone: descriptive, not accusatory. A low disclosure score means little is public. It does not mean a company is using more or less power or water.

Methodology: the 100-point rubric
  • "Not yet checked" criteria count 0 in the total and are marked as such. A company's score may rise once they are checked.
  • Timeliness (C8) is measured from the end of the reported period to publication, and uses the check date of Oct 15, 2026.
  • Scores use only evidence we verified in the linked sources. Apple's score is a partial review: most criteria are unchecked.
  • Planned process (not yet in place): publish the rubric before scores, give each company 14 days to send sources before first publication, and keep a public changelog.
  • Equinix (colocation) and Epoch-estimated sites are shown in the comparison but are not scored.

3 · Company comparison

Reported figures, side by side with their caveats

Not like-for-like. Companies differ in:
  • Boundary: owned/operated sites only (Google, Microsoft) vs including leased (Meta) vs including colocation share (Apple).
  • Fiscal year: Microsoft Jul–Jun (FY25), Apple Oct–Sep, others calendar year.
  • Water: withdrawn vs consumed are different quantities. Per-site Microsoft and Meta figures are withdrawal only.
  • WUE: Meta and AWS divide withdrawn water by IT kWh. Microsoft uses cooling and humidification water. Not comparable.
  • Units: kept as published (MWh, ML, million gallons). We do not convert.
Compare each company with its own history rather than with others, and do not add them into a market total.
